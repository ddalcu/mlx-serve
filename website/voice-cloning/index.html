mlx-serve/Deep dive · Voice cloning

Clone any voice from
six seconds of audio.

Record a short clip — or drop one in — and Qwen3-TTS speaks your text in that voice. Zero-shot, no training, no transcript. And because a voice is biometric data, it all runs on your Mac, never through a cloud API.

How Qwen3-TTS works

Record. Type. Listen.

Open the Audio pane, record 6–8 seconds of any voice in-app (or pick a file — reference clips are normalized automatically), type your text, and generate. The model extracts a speaker embedding from the clip — a compact fingerprint of timbre and delivery — and conditions the synthesis on it. No fine-tune, no waiting, no dataset.

The embedding pipeline was validated bit-for-bit against the reference implementation — the clone you get locally is the clone the model's authors shipped.

Why local matters here

Your voice is a password. Treat it like one.

Voice is biometric data — the same signal that unlocks bank phone lines and impersonates you to your family. Cloud cloning services keep your reference uploads on their terms. Here, the recording, the embedding, and every synthesized sample stay on your Mac: no account, no upload, no retention policy to read.

Clone voices you have the right to use — yours, a consenting collaborator's, a character you own. The tool is local; the responsibility is too.

  • No upload — the reference clip never leaves the machine.
  • No account — nothing to subscribe to, nothing to leak.
  • Works offline — once the model is downloaded, airplane mode is fine.

Breeze-TTS-2 server API

Speech, voice design and cloning

24 kHz mono WAV from the built-in breeze_tts plugin. These are HTTP controls, not a Breeze-specific app catalog, Audio pane or Voice Mode integration.

Use a server build containing Breeze support and the complete dense bf16 mlx-community/Breeze-TTS-2-mlx repository. Keep its root config, tokenizer and weight shards together with audio_tokenizer/; without audio_tokenizer/model.safetensors the pack is incomplete. Downloading the pack does not add the plugin to an older server binary.

Download with the Hugging Face CLI, then serve locally
hf download mlx-community/Breeze-TTS-2-mlx \
  --local-dir "$HOME/models/Breeze-TTS-2-mlx"

mlx-serve --model "$HOME/models/Breeze-TTS-2-mlx" \
  --host 127.0.0.1 --port 11234

Once GET /health succeeds, send a request from another terminal. Omit voice for untagged speech, or select S0 through S9 (case-insensitive). These are speaker slots, not Kokoro/OpenAI voice names. instruction describes the voice or delivery; instructions is an alias, with singular taking precedence.

Generate a designed voice
curl --fail-with-body http://localhost:11234/v1/audio/speech \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{
    "input": "Clear skies today.",
    "voice": "S2",
    "instruction": "A calm, warm voice.",
    "seed": 7
  }' \
  --output breeze.wav

This pinned-model example needs no model field. On a multi-model server, add the Breeze id from GET /v1/models. Success is raw 24 kHz mono, 16-bit PCM WAV, not JSON. The non-negative integer seed defaults to 42 and repeats a request with the same model/build.

Cloning is transcript-conditioned: prepare a clean, short 24 kHz mono PCM WAV, put standard base64 of the entire file in ref_audio, and its exact words in non-empty ref_text. Put the new words in input. A filename, URL or MP3 is not a reference upload. Normalize the clip yourself rather than relying on automatic resampling; the first clone request also loads the Mimi encoder. Only clone voices you have permission to use.

max_frames optionally caps codec frames per text segment, not the whole request (12.5 frames/s). EOS, the text estimate and context can shorten a take; a small cap can truncate it. Long text is segmented and joined, which can reset prosody. stream: true returns SSE progress at stage breeze and a final complete event containing base64 WAV, not playable PCM chunks. Handle SSE error events even after HTTP 200.

Unknown fields such as language, temperature and warm_only are refused by name. Breeze infers language from text. The common speed and response_format keys do not change Breeze's generated rate or WAV output. See the API summary and complete guide for cloning commands, request fields and troubleshooting.

License and validation: the weights are for research and non-commercial use only; read the model's terms. The server's MIT license does not relicense them. Breeze has synthetic conformance and real-pack Mimi encoder parity checks, not an end-to-end reference-synthesis or clone-quality oracle; the Qwen3-TTS validation claims on this page do not apply to Breeze.

FAQ

Voice cloning, answered

How much reference audio do I need?

For Qwen3-TTS in the app, a few seconds — 6–8 is plenty; clips are normalized automatically and no transcript is needed. For the Breeze API, use a short clean reference, normalize it to 24 kHz mono PCM WAV yourself, and send its matching transcript. Longer Breeze references consume more context.

Do I need a transcript of the reference audio?

It depends on the backend. Qwen3-TTS Base extracts a speaker embedding from the clip alone. Breeze-TTS-2 requires both ref_audio and a matching non-empty ref_text. The new words to speak go in input, not ref_text.

Is this a fine-tune? How long does "cloning" take?

No fine-tune — it's zero-shot. Qwen3-TTS computes a speaker embedding from your clip. Breeze conditions on encoded reference audio and its transcript; the first clone request also loads its audio encoder. Neither needs a training run.

How good is the clone, really?

The speaker-encoder port is bit-exact against the reference implementation, so fidelity matches what the Qwen3-TTS authors published. Quality scales with the reference clip: clean, close-mic audio clones better than a noisy room.

Whose voice am I allowed to clone?

Yours, and anyone's who gave you permission. Running locally means no service is policing you — which means the judgment is yours. Don't impersonate people.
